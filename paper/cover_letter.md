*Quantitative Imaging in Medicine and Surgery*

Dear Editor,

I submit for your consideration an Original Article entitled **"Two more
landmarks: identifiability of the three-dimensional Cobb angle measured from
biplanar radiographs"**.

**What the paper reports.** A three-dimensional Cobb angle computed from the
endplate landmarks of a frontal and a lateral radiograph is an attractive
quantity, because an automatic detector already produces those landmarks. The
paper shows that it is not identifiable as usually formulated: four endplate
corners per view supply two constraints on the three rotations that define a
vertebra's orientation, so axial rotation is unconstrained and the recovered
endplate normal is in error by a median of 5.6° across the anatomical range,
rising linearly with the rotation present. Adding the two pedicle centroids,
already visible on the frontal view and the basis of the Nash-Moe and
Perdriolle gradings, closes the system exactly. The paper then states what a
detector would have to deliver — a 90th-percentile residual below one degree
requires the pedicles placed to about 0.85 mm — and shows that patient-specific
pedicle geometry is unnecessary, reporting a normative table measured from 394
vertebrae that leaves a median residual of 0.27° when it is wrong by 2 mm.

**Why this journal.** The result is a measurement-theory finding with
practical numbers attached, rather than a clinical series or a detector
architecture, and it is addressed to the people building automated
measurement rather than to surgeons. That is the readership *QIMS* serves.

**Ethics — a point I would like to raise explicitly.** This study analysed
only computational phantoms and the publicly available, de-identified VerSe CT
collection. No patients were recruited, no identifiable data were accessed,
and no new human or animal data were generated, so institutional review board
approval and informed consent were not applicable rather than waived. I note
that the submission checklist is worded for studies that did obtain approval
and consent, and I did not wish to affirm that wording where it does not
describe the work. The Ethical Statement in the manuscript sets out the
position accurately, and it carries the accountability sentence the journal
requires verbatim. **[TBC: if the editorial office would prefer different
